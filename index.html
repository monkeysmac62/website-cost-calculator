How the numbers work

The renewal cliff, explained

Most shared hosting is sold the way gym memberships are: a low price to get you in the door, then the regular price once you are a member. Namecheap’s Stellar plan is a clear example — $26.88 for the first year, then $55.88 a year after that. The domain name works the same way: the first year is often discounted, and the renewal settles at the regular rate of about $15 a year. None of this is hidden, but it is easy to miss, because the big number on the sales page is always the first-year price. That is why this calculator shows Year 2 next to Year 1. The honest cost of a website is not the first bill — it is the bill you keep paying.

Prices change — hosts run sales and raise renewal rates — so treat every number here as a planning figure and check the merchant’s page before buying.

Why is the DIY first year so cheap?

Three things keep the first-year number small. First, the software is free: WordPress itself costs nothing, and thousands of free themes and plugins cover the basics. Second, hosting companies compete hard for new customers, so the first year is sold below cost — the $26.88 Stellar plan is the clearest example. Third, the things people assume are expensive are often free: an SSL certificate costs $0, and a basic backup plugin costs $0 too. What you are really paying for in Year 1 is a domain name, a place to host the files, and optionally an email mailbox and a premium theme. That is the whole list — there is no hidden fourth item.

Frequently asked questions

Is SSL free?

Yes. An SSL certificate — the thing that puts the padlock in the browser — is free through Let’s Encrypt, and almost every shared host installs and renews it for you automatically. You should never pay for a basic SSL certificate on a personal blog or small business site. Paid certificates exist, but they are for sites that need extended validation or a warranty, which a small site does not.

Where do I buy a domain?

From a domain registrar — a company licensed to sell domain names. Namecheap is one of the best-known options, and the buy buttons on this page go to its domain, hosting, and email pages. You can also buy a domain from your hosting company, but keeping the domain with a registrar and the hosting with a host makes it easier to move later. A .com usually costs $13.98 for the first year and about $15 a year after that.

Why is hosting so much cheaper in the first year than at renewal?

Because the first year is a promotional price. Hosts discount the first term to win new customers, then charge the regular rate when you renew — for the Stellar plan, that means $26.88 the first year and $55.88 a year after. Some hosts raise prices by two or three times at renewal. It is not a trick, but it is the single most common surprise for new site owners, which is why this calculator shows the renewal number up front.

Can I build it myself if I have no technical skills?

Yes, for a blog, a portfolio, or a small five-page business site. WordPress powers a large share of the web precisely because it does not require coding: you pick a theme, add pages, and write content in an editor that works like a word processor. The parts that feel technical — pointing the domain at the host and installing WordPress — are usually one-click steps in the host’s dashboard, and most hosts have free support chat if you get stuck. An online store is the one case where you may want help, because payments and shipping add real complexity.

How much will I pay in the second year?

For the basic DIY setup — domain, Stellar hosting, and free SSL — about $71 a year: roughly $15 for the domain and $55.88 for hosting. Add about $12 a year if you keep business email, and about $70 a year if you keep a premium backup plugin. A paid theme is a one-time purchase, so it costs nothing in Year 2. In other words, the second year is cheaper than the first once the theme is paid for — the hosting renewal jump is already included in that $71.
